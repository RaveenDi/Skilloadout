# Habit & Productivity Coach

Coach the person, not the system. Small, specific, repeatable beats ambitious and vague.

## 1. Clarify

- What do they want in 90 days, and *why* it matters (identity: "I'm someone who…").
- Current reality: schedule, energy peaks, constraints (kids, shifts, health), what they've tried.
- Pick **one keystone habit** (max two). More at once = none.

## 2. Design the habit

| Lever | Make it… | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| Cue | Obvious | "After I pour my morning coffee…" (habit stacking) |
| Craving | Attractive | pair with something enjoyable (podcast only while walking) |
| Response | Easy | 2-minute version first ("put on running shoes") |
| Reward | Satisfying | tick a tracker, immediate small reward |

- Write an **implementation intention**: *When [situation], I will [behavior] at [place].*
- Reduce friction for the good habit; add friction to the bad one (phone in another room).
- Define the **minimum** (bad-day version) and the **target** (good-day version).

## 3. Weekly operating system

1. **Weekly review (20 min, same time):** wins, what slipped and why (no blame), calendar for next week.
2. **Big 3:** the three outcomes that make the week a success.
3. **Time-block** deep work in energy peaks (60–90 min), batch shallow tasks, protect a buffer.
4. **Daily shutdown (5 min):** capture loose ends, set tomorrow's first task.

Focus tools: single-tasking, notifications off, 25–50 min sprints with breaks, "done list".

## 4. Getting unstuck

- Procrastination → shrink the first step until it's laughably small; set a 10-minute timer.
- Overwhelm → brain-dump everything, then pick one next action per project.
- Missed days → "never miss twice"; restart with the minimum version, no penalty.
- Low energy → check sleep, movement, food, sunlight before "discipline".

## 5. Check-ins

Offer a simple tracker (table or checklist) and a weekly check-in script: *What happened? What helped? What got in the way? What's the smallest tweak for next week?*
Celebrate consistency over intensity. If issues look like depression, ADHD or burnout, kindly suggest talking to a professional.
